433.920 MHz Flagship platform

M.P.M.R

One 4×4 skid-steer chassis, rebuilt four times. Everything below the deck stays the same: drive, power, sealed equipment case. Everything above it changes with the mission. Scroll to step through the builds.

CONFIG 01/04 STAGE 1 4×4 · SKID-STEER

  1. 01 Stage 1

    Proof the drive platform could carry compute and sensors over rough ground. Xbox Kinect 360 depth for obstacle avoidance, teleoperation, onboard lighting.

  2. 02 GHRS greenhouse

    Autonomous navigation between crop rows, a 6-DOF arm, and an EfficientNetV2-S disease classifier running on the robot: 85.4% accuracy at 0.65 confidence. Diagnosis happens on board, because greenhouses rarely have signal.

  3. 03 Crowd control

    LIDAR and SLAM for mapping, depth, IMU and GPS fused for autonomous patrol with teleop fallback. Person detection, fire detection, and a tracked non-lethal water nozzle. Shown to Jordanian defence and public-security bodies.

  4. 04 SCORP turret

    The current build. A stabilised pan-tilt turret that acquires a moving target and holds it, tested with a non-lethal platform. A 4×4 reconnaissance variant with Jetson Orin autonomy and long-range teleop is in development.

915.000 MHz Tracking

Acquire, follow, hold.

SCORP and my vision-guided turret both solve the same loop: find the target in the frame, measure how far off-centre it is, and drive pan and tilt to close the gap without overshooting into oscillation.

The panel on the right runs a damped spring version of that loop. Your pointer is the target. Hold still and it locks. Leave it alone and it tracks a simulated contact.

  • OpenCV and MediaPipe detection
  • Pan-tilt servo control on a Pi 5
  • Trajectory compensation on the vision turret

2450.000 MHz Bench

Where it gets made.

Off the clock Comet

The original tracking system weighs under two hundred grams.

Comet is my kestrel. A kestrel can hang dead still in a headwind and lock onto a mouse moving in the grass below. It has no LIDAR, no Jetson and no training set. Every tracker I build is chasing what he does without thinking.

I also wrote the app that records what he eats. It runs on a ten-day feeding cycle rather than a week, so the same days don't keep drawing on the same wild prey. It's one HTML file and works on a phone in the garden with no signal.

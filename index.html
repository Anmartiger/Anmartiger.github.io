M.P.M.R
433.920 MHz

      

        
Multi-Purpose Mobile Robot. One 4×4 skid-steer chassis, rebuilt four times. Everything below the deck stays the same: drive, power, sealed equipment case. Everything above it changes with the mission.

        

          
          
          
          
        

        

          
          
Stage 1
Proof the drive platform could carry compute and sensors over rough ground. Xbox Kinect 360 depth for obstacle avoidance, teleoperation, onboard lighting.

            

        

        

          
          
GHRS greenhouse
Autonomous navigation between crop rows, a 6-DOF arm, and an EfficientNetV2-S disease classifier running on the robot: 85.4% accuracy at 0.65 confidence. Diagnosis happens on board, because greenhouses rarely have signal.

            

        

        

          
          
Crowd control
LIDAR and SLAM for mapping, with depth, IMU and GPS fused for autonomous patrol and teleop fallback. Person detection, fire detection, and a tracked non-lethal water nozzle. Shown to Jordanian defence and public-security bodies.

            

        

        

          
          
SCORP turret
The current build. A stabilised pan-tilt turret that acquires a moving target and holds it, tested with a non-lethal platform. At FB Group I also led the design of a 4×4 reconnaissance rover with Jetson Orin autonomy and long-range teleop.

            

        

        

          
          

            
Ground control. The operator dashboard for the rover: RPLIDAR C1 polar map, camera feed, heading, pitch and roll, GPS fix and track log, live drive control over GPIO.

AUTO · SEARCH

          

          

            
Acquire, follow, hold.

            
SCORP and the vision-guided turret solve the same loop: find the target in the frame, measure how far off-centre it is, drive pan and tilt to close the gap without overshooting into oscillation.

            
This panel runs a damped-spring version of that loop. Your pointer is the target. Hold still and it locks. Leave it alone and it tracks a simulated contact.

            
  • OpenCV and MediaPipe detection
  • Pan-tilt servo control on a Pi 5
  • Projectile trajectory compensation

Comet
Off the clock

      

        

          
          

            
The original tracking system weighs under two hundred grams.

            
Comet is my kestrel. A kestrel can hang dead still in a headwind and lock onto a mouse moving in the grass below, with no LIDAR, no Jetson and no training set. Every tracker on this deck is chasing what he does without thinking.

            
His feeding log runs on a ten-day cycle rather than a week, so the same days don't keep drawing on the same wild prey. It's one HTML file and works on a phone in the garden with no signal.
